Medicare near Fort Bliss (El Paso)

Why the Medicare choice is different for a Fort Bliss family

El Paso’s military-retiree community reaches 65 with TRICARE For Life, which requires Medicare Parts A and B, pays secondary to Medicare and carries creditable pharmacy coverage. A Medigap policy and a Part D plan are usually redundant for a TFL family; the open question is whether an MA-only Advantage plan’s extras are worth it, and whether its network reaches the Las Cruces doctors many El Pasoans use.

William Beaumont Army Medical Center sees retirees on a space-available basis; Medicare does not pay there. The El Paso VA Health Care System serves veterans without TFL, for whom the Part B timing rule is the one that matters.

When you can enroll

Turning 65? Your Initial Enrollment Period is the 7 months around your birthday. Already on Medicare? The Annual Election Period is Oct 15–Dec 7, and Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment runs Jan 1–Mar 31. A plan leaving your county opens a Special Enrollment Period.

Questions from Fort Bliss (El Paso) families

Frequently asked questions

I retired from Fort Bliss with TRICARE For Life. Do I need anything besides Medicare A and B?

Usually not. TFL fills Medicare’s gaps and its pharmacy is creditable. An MA-only Advantage plan can add dental or vision; we check whether one is worth it and whether its network reaches your doctors.

Does Medicare pay at William Beaumont?

No. WBAMC sees retirees on a space-available basis under TRICARE rules. Most Fort Bliss retirees pair TFL with civilian care in El Paso or Las Cruces.

I use the El Paso VA. Do I need Part B?

In most cases, yes. VA care is not creditable for Part B; delaying it triggers a permanent penalty.
